Thank you for reading my manifesto!!

#### Tanish Gupta

Hi everyone! I’m a first-year Computer Science student at Warwick, and I’m running to be a Maths Society Fresher Representative.

What I enjoy most about mathematics is the process of figuring things out: approaching a difficult problem from different angles, understanding why a solution works, and discovering how an abstract idea can be applied to something tangible. That curiosity has shaped both my academic interests and the projects I work on, from algorithm visualisation and performance comparisons to developing a C++ matching engine that explores the role of algorithms and data structures in financial markets.

Outside the classroom, I’ve led a competitive maths club, founded a programming club and tutored other students. These experiences have taught me that some of the best learning happens when people exchange ideas, work through challenging problems together and feel comfortable asking questions. That is the kind of environment I’d love to help build at Warwick.

1. Make problem-solving a shared experience

I’d love to help organise collaborative problem-solving sessions, team challenges and friendly competitions that give members the chance to tackle interesting mathematical problems together. From logic and combinatorics to probability and optimisation, these activities could offer something for different interests and experience levels. The emphasis would be on exploring different approaches, learning from one another and enjoying the challenge, rather than simply getting the right answer first.

2. Connect mathematics to the things we build

Mathematics becomes especially exciting when you see what it makes possible. My own technical projects have shown me how mathematical thinking connects to algorithms, efficient data structures, simulation and quantitative finance. I’d love to help bring these connections into the society through informal workshops, demonstrations and interdisciplinary activities, potentially collaborating with other student societies. Whether someone is interested in pure mathematics, computer science, economics or something entirely different, there is always another application or idea worth exploring.

3. Make it easier for people to find their place

A great society should be somewhere people want to return to, not just somewhere they attend an occasional event. Drawing on my experience in tutoring and student leadership, I’d like to help make activities welcoming to newcomers, encourage peer-to-peer learning and create more opportunities for members to meet people with similar interests. You shouldn’t need to be the strongest mathematician in the room to feel that you belong.

4. Turn good ideas into events that actually happen

I would bring initiative, organisation and a willingness to get involved in the practical work behind events. I’d also want to hear directly from members about what they would find useful or enjoyable, work with the existing committee, and help develop ideas that are both realistic and worth attending. A successful society is built through consistent contributions, not just ambitious promises.

*Why me?*

I bring a combination of mathematical curiosity, technical experience and a genuine interest in helping others learn. From leading clubs and tutoring students to building projects that challenge me to think more deeply, I’ve learned to take ideas beyond the initial concept and put them into practice.

As a FREP, I wouldn’t expect to arrive with all the answers. I’d want to listen, contribute, learn from the committee and help make the society better for the people who are part of it.

My aim is simple: to help make Warwick Maths Society a place where we challenge ourselves, discover new ideas and enjoy figuring things out together.

I’d love your support!

#### Thomas-Elliot Miall

As a first-year student at Warwick, I understand the steep transition we are all navigating. I am running for Freshers’ Representative because I want to help foster an environment where we can push each other’s curiosity and challenge our thinking. The Warwick Maths Society is the perfect place to combine our analytical work with a dedicated community, and my primary mission will be to genuinely understand our cohort's needs and advocate for them.

To support my fellow first years, I will focus on two core initiatives:

Module selection sessions; to help us make the jump to university maths and employment, we require rigorous, informed decision-making—especially when choosing optional modules, it’s incredibly easy to not know which modules are required for the future, effectively blocking entire careers if done badly. Therefore, to help everyone navigate this challenge, I will organise Module Selection Ask Me Anythings. By bringing in older students to explain the realities and subtleties of optional modules, we can provide crucial, data-driven insight to help first-years make the best choices for their academic paths and properly insight every student into knowing what prerequisite modules are needed.

Monthly Maths Games Nights; my background in combinatorial game theory and chess has shown me that mathematical thinking is also deeply social and strategic. I plan to introduce a monthly maths games night (featuring board games, chess, and poker). This will provide a relaxed, inclusive environment for us to build our community, share ideas, and find the same joy in problem-solving outside the Zeeman building.

Why vote for me? I have the practical experience to turn these ideas into reality. As head of logistics and co-president for a 250+ person MUN conference, I am highly experienced in meeting with other organizations, and managing strict deadlines. I know that the foundation of any good agreement is understanding each party's needs, and I will lead with clear communication and logic when representing our cohort to the exec team.

I would massively value the chance to contribute to this environment, learn from my peers, and ensure every first-year has the resources and community they need to thrive.

Vote Thomas-Elliott Miall for a connected, curious, and supported first-year cohort!
